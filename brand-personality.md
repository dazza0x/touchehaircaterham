## Part A — The Character Archetype

### Meet Helen

Every brand benefits from a human anchor — a single character whose voice, instincts and manner represent the brand at its best. For Touché, that person is **Helen**.

Helen isn't a fictional mascot. She's a composite of what Touché genuinely is: the accumulated character of four decades of craft, community and care in Caterham. When writing any piece of content — a website headline, a social caption, a booking confirmation — ask yourself: *would Helen say this?*

---

### Who Helen Is

Helen is 52. She's been doing hair since her mid-twenties and has been in Caterham so long she's as much a part of the place as the High Street itself. She didn't set out to stay — she just never wanted to leave.

She knows the names of her clients' children, their dogs, their preferred coffee order. She remembers what someone had done six months ago without looking at the notes. She asks about the holiday you mentioned in passing last time. She notices when someone seems a bit off and knows when to chat and when to leave them in peace.

She has strong opinions — about colour, about technique, about how a consultation should be done — but she holds them lightly in conversation. She'll guide you toward something better than what you asked for without ever making you feel you got it wrong. Her compliments mean something because she doesn't scatter them.

She reads the trade press. She goes to training. She knows exactly what's trending and has a considered view on all of it — what will last, what's a phase, what's worth learning. She keeps up not because she has to but because she genuinely cares about the craft.

She's been asked to work in London more than once. She's never been tempted.

She wouldn't write "OMG." She wouldn't say "we're passionate about your journey." She wouldn't post five exclamation marks in a row. She would give you an honest answer, do the job properly, and send you out the door looking better than when you came in.

### What Helen Sounds Like

These are phrases Helen would naturally say. They're not scripts — they're a register calibration.

**In consultation:**
> "Right, let's talk about what you're after. Tell me about your routine — how much time do you actually have in the morning?"

> "I'd steer you slightly warmer than that — it'll suit your skin tone better and grow out much more gracefully. But it's your call."

> "That'll take two sessions to do it properly. We could try to rush it but I wouldn't recommend it."

**On the salon:**
> "We've been here since 1985. We've seen every trend. Some we loved, some we didn't miss. We're still here."

> "A consultation before every appointment — we don't skip that. It's how we make sure we get it right."

> "All prices include VAT and a decent coffee. We think that should be the standard, not the exception."

**On results:**
> "You'll leave looking and feeling incredible. That's what we're here for."

> "We won't just tell you what you want to hear. If something's not right for your hair, we'll say so."

**On the community:**
> "We've styled three generations of some Caterham families. That doesn't happen by accident."

---

### What Helen Would Never Say

| Don't | Why |
|-------|-----|
| *"OMG we are OBSESSED with this look! 😍😍😍"* | Too casual, performative |
| *"Book NOW before slots run out!"* | Pressure-selling; not her style |
| *"We're passionate about your hair journey"* | Vague wellness-speak |
| *"Revolutionary new technique"* | Overblown; she'd just say what it does |
| *"Hey babe, loving your vibe"* | Wrong register entirely |
| *"Best-in-class seamless results"* | Corporate jargon; she'd just show you the work |
| *"Reach out to connect with our team"* | Not how a Caterham salon speaks |

---

### The Helen Test

Before publishing any piece of content, run it through these three questions:

1. **Would Helen say this?** — Does it sound like a real, warm, skilled professional from Caterham? Or does it sound like a generic salon brand on Instagram?
2. **Would Helen be embarrassed by this?** — Is it overselling, gushing, or using language that would make a 52-year-old master stylist cringe?
3. **Could a competitor say this?** — If yes, make it more specific, more Touché, more Helen.

## Part B — The Personality Dial

Helen's character stays constant. What shifts is the **dial setting** — how much warmth vs. authority, how formal vs. conversational, how brief vs. expansive — depending on the situation.

Think of it as Helen in different rooms. She's always herself. She just reads the room.

---

### The Four Dials

```
WARMTH      Low  ●————————————  High
AUTHORITY   Low  ————————●————  High
FORMALITY   Low  ————●————————  High
LENGTH      Short ●——————————— Long
```

These four dials shift with context. The charts below show where each should sit for every key situation.

---

### Situation 1 — New Client Enquiry

*Someone has called or emailed for the first time. They may be nervous. They don't know the salon yet.*

```
WARMTH      ●●●●●●●●●●  HIGH — they need to feel welcome immediately
AUTHORITY   ●●●●●●○○○○  MEDIUM-HIGH — reassure with competence
FORMALITY   ●●●●○○○○○○  LOW-MEDIUM — friendly, not stiff
LENGTH      ●●●●●●●○○○  MEDIUM-LONG — take time, don't rush them
```

**❌ Generic version:**
> "Thank you for your enquiry. We offer a range of services. Please call to book."

**✓ Helen's version:**
> "Welcome — really pleased you got in touch. We'd love to have you in. For new clients, we always start with a proper consultation so we can understand your hair, your lifestyle and what you're looking for. For colour services we'll book a quick skin test beforehand — it's just standard practice and means we can get straight to work on the day. Give us a call on 01883 340771 and we'll find you a time."

### The One-Page Gut Check

Before publishing anything, three questions and one test:

**Three questions:**
1. Would Helen say this?
2. Could any other salon in Surrey say this? (If yes — make it more specific)
3. Is the dial in the right position for this situation?

**One rewrite test:**
Take any piece of copy and ask: *"What would Helen actually say if a client asked her this face to face?"*
Write that down. Clean it up slightly. That's usually the right version.
