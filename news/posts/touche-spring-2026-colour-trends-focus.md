> There's a particular kind of morning in Caterham — usually sometime in March/April — where the light changes almost overnight. Suddenly everything looks a little warmer, a little more hopeful. It's exactly the feeling that's shaping hair colour this spring, and honestly, we couldn't be more here for it.

## Warmth Is Having Its Moment

If the cool, ashy tones of recent years felt a little flat on you, spring 2026 is your season. We're seeing a real shift towards golden blondes, honey browns, rich coppers and caramel tones that look like they've been kissed by actual sunlight. These aren't brash or artificial shades — they're soft, lived-in and genuinely flattering on a wide range of skin tones.

The good news is that warm colour tends to be very forgiving. It adds dimension without the harsh contrast that can sometimes age a look.

### Balayage — Still the Best Delivery for This Look

Balayage remains our go-to technique for achieving that natural, sun-warmed effect — and for good reason. Rather than uniform colour from root to tip, balayage places warmth exactly where the sun would naturally lighten your hair: around the face, through the mid-lengths and at the ends. The result is movement, depth and a glow that feels genuinely natural.

It's also one of the lower-maintenance options we offer, which matters for busy lives. That said, "lower maintenance" doesn't mean "never maintain" — more on that in a moment.

### Classic Highlights, Reimagined Warmer

If you've always been a highlights person, don't feel left behind by the balayage conversation. Traditional foil highlights are simply being dialled into warmer territory this spring — think golden wheat rather than platinum, or butterscotch rather than cool beige. Placed well, they frame the face beautifully and work with your natural base rather than against it.

We've been doing a lot of blended highlight and toner combinations at Touché lately that give that multi-tonal warmth without anything looking overdone.

### The Bit Everyone Forgets — Booking Your Refresh

Here's the honest truth: warm tones can fade faster than cooler ones, especially if you're washing your hair frequently or spending time outdoors. Most colour — whether balayage or highlights — will want a refresh at around four to five weeks to keep it looking its best.

We'd always recommend booking your next appointment before you leave the salon. It's not a sales tactic — it's just practical. Availability fills up quickly, particularly as the better weather arrives and everyone suddenly remembers they want to sort their colour out.

Spring is a lovely time to try something a little different. We'd love to help you find it.
