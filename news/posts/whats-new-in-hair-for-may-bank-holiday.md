The Bank Holiday weekend has a particular feeling to it, a collective exhale, the first real stretch of outdoor time, and an almost universal desire to look like you've already been somewhere warm. If you've been meaning to refresh your hair before summer properly arrives, now is the right moment. Here's what's actually happening in the salon right now.

## Lived-in colour is still leading — but it's getting more precise

The effortless, grown-out look isn't going anywhere, but the technique behind it is becoming considerably more refined. Clients want colour that looks natural in week one *and* week twelve. That means careful placement, tonal balance, and increasingly, an attention to what's already in the hair before we start.

There's been genuine industry conversation recently around the importance of metal build-up in the hair affecting colour results. Oligo's colour range, which includes a built-in metal purifier, reflects a broader shift toward pre-treatment awareness. For anyone lightening hair this season, that kind of preparation makes a real difference to evenness and longevity. It's not a gimmick — it's good practice catching up with good product.

For late spring and early summer, the tones we're working with most are warm: honey blondes, soft coppers, and the kind of caramel-touched brunettes that look lit from within. Cool, ashy results still have their place, but the season is pulling things golden.

## The cuts that work hardest right now

Longer layers are having a quiet resurgence, not the heavily textured look of a few years ago, but something more considered. Weight removed in the right places gives movement without sacrificing length, which matters when you're heading into humidity and heat. The bixie — that mid-length between a bob and a pixie — has settled into something genuinely wearable rather than a passing moment.

If you've been sitting on the fence about a fringe, curtain styles continue to be the most-requested new addition we see. They soften without commitment, and they suit an unusually wide range of face shapes.

## Occasion hair — but make it relaxed

Bank Holidays mean events: weddings in the diary, garden parties, long lunches. What clients want for these occasions has shifted. The overly structured updo has largely given way to something softer — textured low buns, loose braided elements, styles that look considered but not rigid. The goal is hair that still looks like *you*, just better.

## A word on consultation

Whatever you're planning a colour refresh, new cut, or a rethink of direction, the conversation before we pick up a brush or scissors matters enormously. Getting the result right means understanding where you're starting from, not just where you want to go. If you're uncertain what would suit you this season, book a consultation. That's what they're for.

Spring is short. Make the most of it.
